A sweep line processes segment endpoints and intersections from left to right. When vertical segments occur, we can tilt the figure slightly: keep the coordinate axes fixed, leave each point's height unchanged, and add a fixed multiple of its height to its horizontal coordinate. Alternatively, we can keep the geometric points fixed and describe them in an oblique coordinate system.

This transformation is called a **shear**:

$$
\teachViolet{x'}=x+sy,\qquad \teachViolet{y'}=y.
$$

Here $s$ is the shear coefficient. These articles explain why we can choose

$$
\boxed{s=\frac{1}{256(M+1)^5}}.
$$

We must distinguish two things: **exact rational arithmetic and an invertible transformation keep intersection results correct; a sufficiently small coefficient prevents the original horizontal order of events from reversing.** Only the latter requires the bounds below.

These articles use only fractions, absolute values, inequalities, and pairs of linear equations in two unknowns. Read $x'$ as "the new horizontal coordinate"; the prime simply distinguishes new coordinates from old ones. $|a|$ is the distance from $a$ to $0$, for example $|-3|=3$; $M^5$ means multiplying five copies of $M$.

Keep in mind the two questions the proof must answer: **How far apart must two events originally be horizontally? By how much can the shear reduce that distance?** If the reduction is less than the original gap, their left-to-right order cannot reverse. We will calculate $M$ from the input endpoints below; it is not an arbitrarily chosen constant.
